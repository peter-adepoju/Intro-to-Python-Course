Week 12, Day 5

Phase 1 exam

This is the end of Phase 1. The exam checks that you can use everything from Weeks 1 to 11 on your own: a quiz on the ideas, eight programming problems, and one larger program. It’s marked by the same checks as every exercise, but there are no hints and no solutions until tomorrow’s debrief. Take it seriously, and take it honestly: it’s for you.

By the end of today you can

  • Show you can solve unseen problems with Phase 1 tools, without hints
  • Manage your time across problems of different sizes
  • Find out exactly which topics need more work before Phase 2

Before you start

The rules. You have 2 hours and 50 minutes in total, the length of a normal day. Work alone. Close your notes, don’t use the tutor, and don’t search the web: the point is to find out what you can do, and the debrief can only help with gaps it can see. You may run your code as often as you like and use Check my answer as often as you like.

The marks.

Part What Marks
A 20 quiz questions 20 (1 each)
B 8 problems 110
C one larger program 50
Total 180

For a problem, count its full marks only when every check passes. A score of 108 (60%) passes Phase 1; 144 (80%) is a distinction. If you pass, you’re ready for Phase 2. If you don’t, tomorrow’s debrief tells you what to revise, and you can take the exam again after a few days.

Strategy. Read every problem first. Do the ones you’re sure of first, and don’t spend more than 20 minutes stuck on one: move on and come back. Partial programs earn nothing here, but they might in your notes for the debrief, so keep your attempts.

Start the timer when you’re ready.

Part B: eight problems

Exercise10 points
B1: Phone numbers

Nigerian mobile numbers are written in several ways: 0803 123 4567, 0803-123-4567, +234 803 123 4567 or 2348031234567. Write normalise(number) that returns the number in the form +2348031234567. Ignore spaces and dashes. After them, a valid number is either 0 followed by 10 digits, 234 followed by 10 digits, or +234 followed by 10 digits, where the 10 digits don’t start with 0. Raise a ValueError with the message invalid number: and the original text (with quotes, as {number!r} gives) for anything else.

Exercise10 points
B2: Runs

Write runs(text) that returns a list of (character, count) tuples for each run of the same character: runs("aaabccdd") is [("a", 3), ("b", 1), ("c", 2), ("d", 2)]. Then write expand(pairs) that turns such a list back into the text.

Exercise10 points
B3: Predict

Work out each value without running anything. Each one is worth getting right; all five are needed for the marks.

Exercise15 points
B4: Merge

Write merge(a, b) that takes two lists already sorted from smallest to largest and returns one sorted list of all their items, without using sort, sorted, min or max. Work through both lists once, always taking the smaller front item.

Exercise15 points
B5: Spiral

Write spiral(grid) that returns the items of a rectangular grid in spiral order: along the top row left to right, down the right column, along the bottom row right to left, up the left column, and so on inwards. Don’t change the grid.

1 2 3
4 5 6    ->  [1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 8, 7, 4, 5]
7 8 9
Exercise15 points
B6: Attendance

Each line of register.txt holds a date, a space, and the names of the students present that day separated by commas (possibly with spaces after the commas, possibly with nobody). Write attendance(path) that returns a dictionary from each student who appears at least once to the percentage of days they were present, rounded to 1 place. Blank lines are ignored and don’t count as days.

register.txt
2026-10-05 Ada, Bo, Cy
2026-10-06 Ada,Cy

2026-10-07 Bo, Cy
2026-10-08
2026-10-09 Ada, Bo, Cy, Di
Bug hunt15 points
B7: Three bugs

top_words(text, n) should return the n most common words of at least 3 letters (ignoring case and the punctuation .,!?), as a list of (word, count) pairs, most common first, ties in alphabetical order. It has three bugs. Find and fix them.

Exercise20 points
B8: Valid brackets

Write check_brackets(text) that checks the brackets (), [] and {} in a piece of text. Other characters are ignored. Return "OK" if every bracket is closed by the matching kind in the right order. Otherwise return the problem found first, reading from left to right, with the position (counting from 0):

  • unexpected ) at 4 for a closing bracket with nothing open;
  • expected ] but found ) at 7 for a closing bracket of the wrong kind;
  • ( at 2 never closed at the end, for the last bracket opened that was never closed.

Part C: a larger program

Exercise50 points
C: The car park

A car park records every car entering and leaving in parking.csv, in time order. Each row has a plate, a time (HH:MM, all on one day) and an event (in or out).

Charges: the first 30 minutes are free. After that, the charge is 200 naira for every hour or part of an hour, counted from the moment the car entered, up to a maximum of 1500 naira. So a stay of 30 minutes is free, 31 minutes costs 200, 60 minutes costs 200, 61 minutes costs 400, and anything over 7 hours costs 1500.

Write:

  • minutes(time): "09:05" gives 545 (minutes after midnight). Raise a ValueError for anything that isn’t a valid HH:MM time.
  • fee(stay): the charge for a stay of stay minutes.
  • process(path): reads the file and returns a tuple (charges, problems):
  • charges is a list of (plate, minutes_stayed, fee) tuples, in the order the cars left;
  • problems is a list of messages, in file order: row N: bad time 'TEXT' for an invalid time, row N: PLATE left without entering for an out with no matching in, row N: PLATE entered twice for an in when that car is already inside (the first entry time is kept), row N: unknown event 'TEXT'; and, after all the rows, PLATE never left for each car still inside, sorted by plate. Rows are numbered from 2 (the header is row 1). Plates are compared in capitals without spaces, and reported that way.
  • The main program processes parking.csv and prints one line per charge: the plate left-aligned in 10, the minutes right-aligned in 6, the text min, and the fee right-aligned in 8 (as in LAG123AA 25 min 0); then Total: N naira; then Problems: and each problem indented by two spaces (or Problems: none).
